\noindent\textit{Short title:} Unalignable mammalian arms races\\[0.3em]
\noindent\textit{One-Sentence Summary:} Joint alignment and biophysical selection localize mammalian genetic conflict to viral interfaces while host hubs stay constrained.

\begin{abstract} \bfseries \boldmath
Antagonistic coevolution between mammalian hosts and viral pathogens generates rapid insertions, deletions, intrinsically disordered tripwires, and gene loss across innate immunity that routinely disrupt sequential comparative phylogenomics. Here we pair statistical homology marginalization, local reticulation routing, and regularized biophysical selection across 25 pre-registered mammalian loci with experimental co-structures and 7,045 canonical orthogroups. Within 13 conflict complexes, diversifying and two-state toggle selection concentrates at direct atomic partner contacts ($42.47\%$ versus $29.18\%$ buried core) and exceeds hijacked host hubs by $14.32$-fold. Joint alignment marginalization prunes $279$ rigid-alignment artifacts across flexible loops, viral protease sensors, and tandem zinc-finger repeats while resolving $10$ gene-conversion tracts, electrostatic charge switches, and $96$ relaxed branches alongside $7$ repaired pseudogenes. Proteome-wide, antiviral effectors double the episodic selection and indel plasticity of hijacked hubs.
\end{abstract}

\noindent
Evolutionary conflict between mammalian innate immunity and viral antagonists produces the fastest-evolving protein surfaces in vertebrate genomes \cite{daugherty2012rules,enard2016viruses}. Under antagonistic coevolution, host receptors and restriction factors accumulate recurrent amino-acid replacements along localized binding patches while the surrounding structural core remains conserved. Classical codon-substitution tests detect those rapidly diverging residues by asking whether nonsynonymous replacements outnumber synonymous changes along a fixed phylogeny \cite{goldman1994codon,muse1994likelihood,pond2005not,murrell2012detecting}. Standard comparative pipelines rely on three consecutive assumptions to evaluate those tests: inactivated alleles containing premature stop codons or frameshifts are removed before analysis, an intact multiple sequence alignment is frozen by progressive heuristic tools \cite{katoh2013mafft,loytynoja2008phylogeny}, and every codon site evolves along a single bifurcating tree. Although convenient for conserved metabolic enzymes, this sequential division of labor collapses across host--virus battlegrounds where evolutionary innovation alters length, topology, and reading frame alongside point substitutions \cite{fletcher2010indelible,jordan2012effects}.

Across mammalian defense loci, four biological mechanisms violate those sequential assumptions at the same time. Rapid insertion--deletion remodeling inside surface-accessible binding loops and intrinsically disordered viral protease tripwires shifts positional homology across species, so rigid aligners pack non-homologous residues into shared columns and fabricate bursts of apparent positive selection \cite{chavarria2016proteolytic,tsu2021diverse,fletcher2010indelible}. Tandemly repeated specificity domains undergo frequent copy-number expansion and contraction that shear positional registers across mammals \cite{oliver2009prdm9,patel2016prdm9gene}. Short-tract paralogous gene conversion transfers divergent motifs between neighboring genes or clades, causing a single tree to misread shared ancestral blocks as convergent adaptation. Finally, discarding pseudogenized orthologues and measuring only overall rate acceleration hides evolution by subtraction alongside conserved-rate toggling between restrictive side chains \cite{mcewan2009truncated,braun2015loss,munoz2011orphan,tenthorey2020mutational}.

To evaluate genetic conflict without these bottlenecks, we applied a unified statistical alignment and selection engine, WALRUS \cite{kosakovsky2026walrus}, together with Empirical Bayes Ridge property-informed selection inference across unaligned mammalian coding sequences \cite{methods}. Instead of conditioning inference on a single heuristic point-estimate alignment, the unified framework preserves frameshifted and premature-stop orthologues, integrates codon likelihoods across a statistical homology ensemble inferred by Inside--Outside optimization, routes statistically supported segmental transfers onto local subtree prune-and-regraft paths within an Ohmic network, and accounts for third-codon-position base-composition drift. Using site-level biophysical regression under conservative shrinkage, we also evaluated selective changes along five physicochemical axes: isoelectric point, helix propensity, sheet propensity, volume, and hydrophobicity. Throughout this framework, alignment ambiguity, topological reticulation, and sequence erosion enter the statistical model simultaneously before site or branch calls are made \cite{kosakovsky2026walrus,methods}.

We paired this methodological formulation with a pre-registered, two-tier mammalian study designed to prevent circular selection of canonical immune genes \cite{methods}. Before running any evolutionary test, an automated screen intersected human Swiss-Prot entries annotated for antiviral defense or host--virus binding against $15,063$ Protein Data Bank (PDB) co-structures. This objective screen locked $25$ Tier-1 loci across four arms: $14$ antiviral effectors and sensors, $5$ viral entry receptors, the tandem-repeat meiotic regulator \textit{PRDM9}, and $5$ cellular host hubs hijacked by HIV-1, Influenza A, or coronaviruses as negative controls. Each locus entered analysis directly from unaligned DNA sequences across $50$ to $80$ mammals ($22$ simian primates for \textit{APOBEC3G}). To test whether patterns across these $25$ loci generalize proteome-wide, we analyzed $7,045$ canonical orthogroups from the mammalian TOGA2 catalog \cite{kirilenko2023integrating}, comparing antiviral effectors against hijacked cellular hubs and the background proteome \cite{methods}.

\subsection*{Direct atomic contacts diverge between conflict interfaces and hijacked hubs}

Reconstructing joint codon homology and selection across the $25$ unaligned Tier-1 datasets recovered substantial structural plasticity alongside strict artifact filtering (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1} and table~S1). Across the entire panel, joint homology inference resolved $2,775$ non-human inserted codon columns and $7,427$ branch insertion or deletion events while maintaining high alignment posterior certainty ($0.705$ to $1.000$) and removing $279$ rigid-alignment false positives that emerge when fixed progressive alignments force incompatible loop codons into shared columns. Direct comparison against progressive heuristic alignments on \textit{TRIM5} confirms this dual distortion (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1}A and fig.~S2A). Progressive alignment excluded the documented feline pseudogene \cite{mcewan2009truncated}, inflated the locus from $595$ to $650$ columns, and yielded $56$ nominal episodic calls at $p \le 0.05$ ($1$ surviving false-discovery correction). Joint modeling retained all $67$ mammals, condensed the homologous ensemble, and removed $6$ alignment artifacts across the locus. Antiviral effectors and viral entry receptors formed a distinct high-conflict band across the $25$-locus architecture plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1}, B and C), with $15.1\%$ to $34.2\%$ of alignment columns evolving under diversifying, toggling, or convergent selection (Table~\ref{tab:arms_summary}).

Contrasting conflict arms against negative-control host hubs verified that elevated selection signals reflect localized antagonism instead of pervasive noise. Across the five PDB-resolved cellular hubs co-opted by viral pathogens (\textit{PPIA}, \textit{ELOB}, \textit{ANP32A}, \textit{RAE1}, and \textit{YWHAZ}), site selection remained constrained between $0.0\%$ and $12.3\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:arms_summary} and table~S1). None of the five hijacked hubs triggered gene-wide episodic diversifying selection under BUSTED-E ($p = 0.115$ for \textit{PPIA}; $p = 1.000$ for the remaining four loci), and conservative biophysical modeling flagged zero strict two-stage property shifts across all five control alignments (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1}C and fig.~S1). By comparison, all five viral entry receptors and $13$ of $14$ antiviral effectors surpassed the gene-wide detection threshold ($p \le 0.10$; $p \le 0.01$ in $17$ of $19$ conflict loci). \textit{TRIM5} illustrates why site-level and structural integration complements gene-wide tests: its gene-wide statistic remained non-significant ($p = 1.000$) because extreme sequence conservation across the RING, B-box, and coiled-coil scaffold diluted rapid turnover across $21.9\%$ of codons concentrated in its capsid-binding domain \cite{sawyer2005positive,tenthorey2020mutational}.

To evaluate whether statistical selection signatures pinpoint physical interaction surfaces, we mapped every resolved human residue across $19$ non-fusion experimental PDB co-structures by heavy-atom minimum distance ($d_{\min}$) to its viral or nucleic-acid partner before examining evolutionary estimates (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}A, Table~\ref{tab:arms_summary}, and table~S2). Within the $13$ conflict co-structures ($8$ antiviral effectors or sensors and $5$ entry receptors), $42.47\%$ ($127$ of $299$) of direct intermolecular contacts ($d_{\min} \le 4.5$\,\AA) showed significant selection. By contrast, only $29.18\%$ ($1,063$ of $3,643$) of buried structural core residues ($d_{\min} > 8.0$\,\AA) met selection thresholds (Fisher's exact odds ratio $\mathrm{OR} = 1.79$, $p = 1.88 \times 10^{-6}$; paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00171$). Strong contact enrichment spanned diverse antagonists and pathogens across intracellular and entry compartments. Intracellular complexes enriched sharply on \textit{APOBEC3H} with HIV-1 Vif ($\mathrm{OR} = 4.08$, $p = 0.019$), \textit{STAT2} with Dengue NS5 ($\mathrm{OR} = 2.10$, $p = 0.031$), and \textit{IRF3} with Rotavirus NSP1 ($\mathrm{OR} = 3.57$, $p = 0.012$) \cite{ito2023a3h,biswal2024stat2,zhao2016irf3}. Viral entry receptors displayed matching interface concentration in \textit{DPP4} with MERS-CoV spike ($\mathrm{OR} = 3.44$, $p = 0.0038$), \textit{NPC1} with Ebola glycoprotein ($\mathrm{OR} = 4.23$, $p = 0.016$), and \textit{ACE2} with SARS-CoV-2 spike ($47.6\%$ versus $29.2\%$, $p = 0.062$) \cite{wang2013dpp4,wang2016npc1,lan2020structure}.

Hijacked cellular hubs displayed evolutionary stasis at their bound viral interfaces. Only $5$ of $102$ ($4.90\%$) direct $\le 4.5$\,\AA\ contacts across \textit{PPIA}, \textit{ELOB}, \textit{ANP32A}, \textit{RAE1}, and \textit{YWHAZ} exhibited selection, matching their own structural cores ($3.56\%$, $p = 0.326$) and trailing conflict contacts by $14.32$-fold ($\mathrm{OR} = 14.32$, $p = 1.52 \times 10^{-14}$; gene-level Mann--Whitney $p = 7.95 \times 10^{-4}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}A) \cite{gamble1996crystal,lu2013elob,arragain2024anp32a,li2021rae1,eisenreichova2022ywhaz}. Pairing host proteins targeted by the same viral pathogens confirmed that divergence tracks host function rather than viral chemistry (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}B). HIV-1 capsid contacts diverge between restriction factor \textit{TRIM5} ($34.15\%$) and co-opted isomerase \textit{PPIA} ($5.0\%$), just as HIV-1 Vif contacts diverge between deaminases \textit{APOBEC3G} and \textit{APOBEC3H} ($50.0\%$) and cofactor \textit{ELOB} ($20.0\%$). Likewise, influenza contacts diverge between \textit{TRIM25} and \textit{ISG15} ($35.5\%$) and nuclear cofactor \textit{ANP32A} ($8.33\%$) \cite{guan2011isg15,daczkowski2017isg15,koliopoulos2018trim25}, while coronavirus partners diverge from $52.3\%$ on \textit{ACE2} and \textit{DPP4} to $0.0\%$ on \textit{RAE1} and \textit{YWHAZ}. Physical architecture also stratified sequence insertions across five structural compartments (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}C). Mean gap fractions remained near zero on hub contacts ($0.57\%$) and conflict cores ($1.40\%$), reached $2.48\%$ on ordered conflict contacts, rose to $16.53\%$ across unmodeled disordered tails ($26.82\%$ selected), and peaked at $35.69\%$ across six dynamic loop and protease-sensor loci ($25.43\%$ selected).

\subsection*{Electrostatic charge inversion and two-state toggling drive ordered escape}

Classical $dN/dS$ models measure only elevated nonsynonymous substitution rates and therefore overlook evolutionary escape along tightly packed protein interfaces where structural geometry permits only restricted biochemical switching \cite{tenthorey2020mutational,mitchell2012evolution}. When we estimated site-specific property weights across the $25$ loci under conservative Ridge regularization, amino-acid isoelectric point emerged as the dominant changing physicochemical axis ($41$ strict two-stage and $390$ marginal sites). Helix propensity ($40$ strict, $267$ marginal) and sheet propensity ($20$ strict, $188$ marginal) formed secondary shifting axes, whereas volume ($17$ strict, $194$ marginal) and hydrophobicity ($12$ strict, $137$ marginal) changed less frequently (fig.~S1). Purifying conservation displayed the opposite polarity: hydrophobicity constrained more than four times as many sites strictly as it changed ($52$ conserved versus $12$ changing), paired with widespread core charge and volume maintenance ($57$ conserved isoelectric-point sites and $30$ conserved volume sites). Across the $13$ conflict co-structures, strict biophysical reprogramming concentrated $4.11$-fold at $\le 4.5$\,\AA\ direct partner contacts ($4.68\%$ versus $1.18\%$ in cores, $p = 5.97 \times 10^{-5}$; marginal enrichment $2.32$-fold, $p = 6.25 \times 10^{-6}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2}C), whereas hijacked cellular hub contacts recorded $0.0\%$ ($0$ of $102$) strict or marginal biophysical shifts.

Individual binding pockets show how exchangeability toggling and charge inversion operate beneath conserved overall substitution rates (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}A). On \textit{APOBEC3G} bound to HIV-1 Vif (PDB 8CX0), standard episodic rate testing identified only the canonical electrostatic switch residue \texttt{D128} ($1$ of $9$ Loop~7 contacts at $p \le 0.05$, $d_{\min} = 2.70$\,\AA) because steric packing along the loop suppresses unrestricted amino-acid diversity \cite{li2023a3g,guo2014vif}. Incorporating amino-acid exchangeability modeling detected six adjacent Loop~7 interface residues evolving via constrained two-state toggling or restricted alphabets. Those calls span aromatic shuffling at \texttt{Y125} and \texttt{F126} ($q = 0.060$), \texttt{W127} ($q = 0.060$), \texttt{D130} ($q = 0.074$), \texttt{Y131} ($q = 0.060$), and \texttt{Q132} ($q = 0.012$), which raises binding-motif sensitivity seven-fold from $1$ to $7$ of $9$ residues (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}A). Comparable hidden escape appeared inside the double-stranded RNA activation cleft of \textit{OAS1} (PDB 4IG8) and the sarbecovirus binding pocket of \textit{ACE2} (PDB 6M0J) \cite{donovan2013oas1,lan2020structure,chan2020engineering}. Both \textit{OAS1} contact residues \texttt{D35} ($d_{\min} = 2.96$\,\AA) and \texttt{R47} ($d_{\min} = 3.01$\,\AA) scored non-significant under rate-only episodic testing ($q \ge 0.937$), yet strict biophysical testing identified strong electrostatic charge inversion at both positions ($\lambda_{\mathrm{pI}} = -2.16$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}} = 4.35 \times 10^{-7}$ at \texttt{D35}; $\lambda_{\mathrm{pI}} = -1.62$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}} = 2.47 \times 10^{-7}$ at \texttt{R47}) driven by restricted toggling. Similarly, \textit{ACE2} residue \texttt{G354} ($d_{\min} = 3.43$\,\AA) sits inside the sarbecovirus salt-bridge pocket and registered overall rate conservation paired with strict isoelectric-point switching ($\lambda_{\mathrm{pI}} = -1.79$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}} = 4.96 \times 10^{-9}$), capturing species-level electrostatic remodeling across mammalian receptor orthologues.

\subsection*{Indel tripwires, gene conversion, and repeat arrays reshape flexible defenses}

Across the six antiviral proteins governed by dynamic surface loops and disordered sensors rather than rigid protein--protein complexes, joint alignment isolated experimentally mapped viral interfaces without inflating gap artifacts. Diversifying and toggle selection enriched cleanly inside \textit{TRIM5} v1--v4 capsid-binding loops ($34.15\%$ versus $19.88\%$ scaffold, $\mathrm{OR} = 2.09$, $p = 0.00392$) and \textit{BST2} cytoplasmic tetherin targets ($27.63\%$ versus $14.55\%$, $\mathrm{OR} = 2.24$, $p = 0.0104$) \cite{tenthorey2020mutational,jia2009species,mcnatt2009species}. On \textit{EIF2AK2}, kinase helix inserts engaged by poxvirus pseudosubstrates displayed matching elevation ($37.78\%$ versus $25.19\%$, $\mathrm{OR} = 1.80$, $p = 0.00933$) \cite{elde2009protein}. On the inflammasome sensor \textit{NLRP1}, whose disordered amino-terminal linker evolves as a cleavage tripwire recognized by enteroviral 3C proteases \cite{chavarria2016proteolytic,tsu2021diverse}, the unified model accommodated $534$ non-human inserted columns and $1,082$ branch indel events ($21.43\%$ loop selection) while homology marginalization stripped out $51$ false-positive calls triggered by local alignment uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}B). At the same time, testing directional base-composition drift at synonymous third codon positions identified significant compositional non-stationarity across $16$ of the $25$ loci (table~S1), preventing mutational nucleotide bias from mimicking amino-acid selection.

Disentangling point selection from reticulate exchange and tandem-repeat slippage prevents two additional sources of false positives in dynamic immune families. Across the $25$ alignments, conductance scanning across the Ohmic network evaluated $393$ candidate segmental bridges and routed $10$ statistically supported recombination tracts onto local alternative topologies before site parameters were optimized ($\Delta \ln L = 11.2$ to $67.3$, permutation $p = 0.002$ to $0.004$; table~S3 and fig.~S2B). These localized transfers span seven conflict loci, led by an ancestral cetacean--laurasiatherian conversion block covering codons $498$--$623$ near the antiviral Loop~L4 of \textit{MX1} ($\Delta \ln L = 67.3$), an \textit{NLRP1} sensor tract ($\Delta \ln L = 47.2$), an \textit{NPC1} primate segment ($\Delta \ln L = 32.3$), and shorter exchanges across \textit{IRF3}, \textit{OAS1}, \textit{TRIM5}, \textit{MAVS}, and \textit{APOBEC3H}. On the tandem-repeat meiotic regulator \textit{PRDM9}, where $28$-residue zinc fingers vary rapidly in copy number across mammals, rigid progressive alignment expanded to $1,467$ codons and dropped both pseudogenized canids (fig.~S2A) \cite{oliver2009prdm9,patel2016prdm9gene}. Joint modeling condensed \textit{PRDM9} into $1,334$ homologous columns across all $50$ mammals, pruned $48$ alignment artifacts, and recovered $7.33$-fold atomic contact enrichment in the human crystal complex (PDB 5EGB, $p = 0.0040$). Within those $12$ repeats, selection concentrated inside $36$ of $48$ ($75.0\%$) DNA-contacting specificity positions ($-1$, $+2$, $+3$, $+6$) against $1$ of $48$ ($2.1\%$) structural zinc-coordinating cysteines and histidines ($\mathrm{OR} = 16.8$, $p = 1.51 \times 10^{-16}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}C).

\subsection*{Lineage-specific gene loss and proteome-wide entrenchment bound host adaptation}

Evolutionary arms races frequently terminate not through continuous amino-acid replacement, but through relaxation of selective constraint and pseudogenization when viral antagonists disappear or defense becomes costly \cite{mcewan2009truncated,braun2015loss,munoz2011orphan}. Admitting unedited coding sequences recovered and frame-repaired all seven documented mammalian pseudogenes across our Tier-1 cohort automatically: feline \textit{TRIM5} ($5$ frameshifts and $2$ internal stop codons repaired), four delphinid \textit{MX1} orthologues (\textit{Delphinus delphis}, \textit{Globicephala melas}, \textit{Orcinus orca}, and \textit{Pseudorca crassidens}), and both canid \textit{PRDM9} orthologues (\textit{Canis lupus} and \textit{Canis aureus}). Branch-level relaxation and loss-of-function Bayes factor testing flagged $96$ branches across the panel (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}D and table~S1). Known pseudogenized clades produced extreme statistical support: in \textit{MX1}, ancestral odontocete cetacean branch \texttt{Node44} ($p = 6.8 \times 10^{-94}$), \textit{Delphinus delphis} ($p = 2.9 \times 10^{-74}$, relaxation parameter $k = 0.061$), and \textit{Orcinus orca} ($p = 2.0 \times 10^{-15}$) stood alongside \textit{Mus musculus} ($p = 2.7 \times 10^{-9}$, $k = 0.532$), agreeing with documented laboratory mouse \textit{Mx1} null alleles \cite{staeheli1986mx}. Both canid \textit{PRDM9} lineages recorded extreme loss-of-function signals ($p \le 1.7 \times 10^{-19}$), alongside clade-specific relaxation in bat \textit{TRIM5} (\textit{Miniopterus fuliginosus}, $p = 5.8 \times 10^{-9}$), cetacean \textit{OAS1} ($p \le 4.3 \times 10^{-15}$), carnivore \textit{NLRP1} ($p \le 1.4 \times 10^{-13}$), and bat and pangolin \textit{IRF3} (\textit{Myotis lucifugus}, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-92}$; \textit{Manis javanica}, $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-27}$).

To test whether the divergence observed across our $25$ Tier-1 loci scales across mammalian proteomes, we evaluated $7,045$ canonical orthogroups processed uniformly from the TOGA2 catalog \cite{kirilenko2023integrating,kosakovsky2026walrus} (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4}, Table~\ref{tab:arms_summary}, and table~S4). Across all three statistical dimensions, antiviral effectors and innate sensors ($n = 49$, Swiss-Prot \texttt{KW-0051}) separated cleanly from hijacked cellular host hubs ($n = 179$, \texttt{KW-0945} excluding \texttt{KW-0051}). Antiviral loci doubled gene-wide episodic diversification ($69.39\%$ versus $39.11\%$, Fisher's exact $p = 1.45 \times 10^{-4}$; proteome background $57.28\%$) and doubled episodic diversifying site density ($12.55\%$ versus $6.09\%$, Mann--Whitney $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-9}$). Simultaneously, codon alignment gap density more than doubled ($15.02\%$ versus $6.73\%$, $p = 1.53 \times 10^{-11}$; $185.86$ versus $118.28$ indel events per $100$ codons; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4}A). Hijacked hubs showed tighter purifying preservation than the background proteome across every metric, retaining orthologues across nearly twice as many mammalian species ($839$ versus $441$ median taxa, $p = 1.85 \times 10^{-7}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4}B) while suppressing toggle and convergent substitutions ($28.34\%$ versus $36.20\%$, $p = 0.00267$). Controlling simultaneously for alignment depth and sequence length via ordinary least-squares regression confirmed independent excess episodic sites ($\beta = +4.82\%$, $\mathrm{SE} = 1.24$, $p = 1.27 \times 10^{-4}$), gap plasticity ($\beta = +3.69\%$, $\mathrm{SE} = 1.14$, $p = 0.00141$), and toggle/convergent turnover ($\beta = +10.29\%$, $\mathrm{SE} = 3.01$, $p = 7.45 \times 10^{-4}$) in antiviral genes relative to hijacked hubs. Antiviral genes also retained $+4.50\%$ excess episodic density against the background proteome after covariate adjustment ($p = 7.87 \times 10^{-7}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4}C and table~S4).

Three analytical boundaries define the scope of these evolutionary conclusions. Where crystal or cryo-EM coordinates omit flexible tails and intrinsically disordered regions---including the carboxyl-terminal prenylation tail of \textit{OAS1} and the enteroviral linker of \textit{NLRP1}---three-dimensional contact auditing alone underestimates pathogen-facing plasticity unless paired with functional loop maps. Across extreme tandem repeats or recently eroded pseudogenes, genuine homology ambiguity lowers local column posterior certainty (mean $0.705$ to $0.754$), so conservative biophysical estimates trade recall for near-zero false-positive rates ($0.0\%$ across all $102$ hijacked hub contacts). Occasional short-branch relaxation flags also occur in hijacked host hubs ($1$ to $6$ branches per locus; table~S1), which reflect lineage-specific gene duplications, retrocopies, or assembly fragmentation rather than whole-locus functional loss. Together, unified homology marginalization, Ohmic reticulation modeling, and regularized biophysical selection resolve why dedicated antiviral sensors innovate along $\le 4.5$\,\AA\ contacts, disordered tripwires, and subtractive paths while hijacked cellular hubs stay entrenched and force viruses toward molecular mimicry.
